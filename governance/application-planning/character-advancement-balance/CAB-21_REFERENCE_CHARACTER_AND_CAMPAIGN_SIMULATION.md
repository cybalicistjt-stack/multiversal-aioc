# CAB-21 — Reference Character & Campaign Simulation

**Program:** CAB — Character Advancement & Balance  
**Tranche:** CAB-21  
**State:** `completed_verified` simulation; standing-delegation decisions recorded separately  
**Owner/final authority:** John Brandon Turner

## 1. Purpose

Stress-test the integrated CAB-20 model using deterministic synthetic Character budgets and Campaign horizons. This tranche validates whether the current **1,300 normal starting XP** and **500 XP/session Standard pacing target** remain coherent with CAB pricing.

The fixtures are QA models, not canonical pregenerated Characters and not claims that every Ability at a price band has identical value. Individual Ability content remains subject to CAB-13/CAB-22 review.

## 2. Simulation assumptions

The simulation uses only already-approved CAB anchors:

- normal starting advancement XP: 1,300;
- five free starting Ability Trees;
- free Tier-1 access in those trees;
- five free eligible Tier-1 Abilities;
- 12 free creation Attribute points after Species modifiers;
- direct Ability reference bands: 250 / 500 / 1,000 / 2,000 / 4,000 / 8,000+ XP;
- ordinary higher-tier access: T2 500, T3 1,250, T4 2,500, T5 5,000 XP;
- extra ordinary progression opening: 1,000 XP;
- Attribute, Skill, Knowledge, Proficiency and HP schedules from CAB-10/CAB-06;
- Standard long-run pacing: 500 XP per substantive session;
- no global XP multipliers;
- no universal Ability-count cap, breadth tax, or readied slots.

## 3. Campaign budget horizons

## 4. Starting-Character budget tests

Normal creation already supplies foundational breadth through five free trees, free Tier-1 access, five free Tier-1 Abilities and the free Attribute pool. The 1,300 XP grant therefore functions as **discretionary direction**, not as the entire Character chassis.

### Fixture A — developing one starting tree

- T2 access in one starting tree: 500 XP;
- one 500-XP direct Ability: 500 XP;
- one 250-XP narrow proficiency/low-burden advancement: 250 XP;
- retained XP: 50.

Result: meaningful early specialization without skipping to advanced depth.

### Fixture B — early statistic specialist

- raise one Attribute from 10 to 13: 1,000 XP cumulative;
- Skill rank 1: 250 XP;
- retained XP: 50.

Result: a noticeable specialist direction while still far from high Attribute or Skill mastery.

### Fixture C — durable start

- creation-authorized +10 direct HP: 1,250 XP cumulative;
- retained XP: 50.

Result: the Character may prioritize durability, but doing so consumes essentially the entire discretionary starting grant. CAB retains the +20 direct-HP cap pending deeper survivability evidence rather than extending this path indefinitely.

### Fixture D — breadth start

- open one additional ordinary progression at Tier 1: 1,000 XP;
- one low-burden 250-XP direct Ability: 250 XP;
- retained XP: 50.

Result: breadth is possible, but it has an explicit opportunity cost and does not add simultaneous Actions by itself.

### Starting-XP conclusion

**1,300 XP remains a sound normal-start default.** It supports one clear discretionary development choice after the free creation chassis, does not cheaply open advanced tiers, and leaves Campaigns free to choose another starting grant explicitly.

## 5. Standard-pacing development fixtures

### 10-session Character — 6,300 total XP

Example statistic specialist:

- Attribute 10→16: 3,500 XP;
- Skill rank 4 cumulative: 2,500 XP;
- retained XP: 300.

This produces a clear specialist after roughly ten substantive sessions without approaching rank-20 Skill mastery or across-the-board Attribute caps.

Example depth specialist:

- T2 + T3 + T4 access in one free starting tree: 4,250 XP;
- one 2,000-XP significant Ability: 2,000 XP;
- retained XP: 50.

A Character can reach Expert access by concentrating heavily, but most of the budget is committed to that path.

### 25-session Character — 13,800 total XP

Opening T2 through T5 access in one free starting tree costs 9,250 XP before later-tier Ability purchases. The Character retains 4,550 XP for actual Abilities, statistics, Skills or other advancement.

This allows a deliberately concentrated Character to begin operating at Apex depth around this horizon, but not to collect multiple fully developed Apex progressions cheaply.

### 50-session Character — 26,300 total XP

One high-stat/knowledge fixture:

- Attribute 10→20: 15,500 XP;
- Knowledge through step 5: 7,750 XP;
- narrow proficiency through Mastery: 1,750 XP;
- retained XP: 1,300.

One multi-progression access fixture:

- T2→T5 access in one free starting tree: 9,250 XP;
- open one additional ordinary progression and access it through T5: 10,250 XP;
- remaining direct-Ability/stat/Skill budget: 6,800 XP.

At 50 Standard sessions, substantial breadth or deep specialization is possible, but neither produces universal mastery.

### 100-session Character — 51,300 total XP

A single Skill reaches rank 19 for 47,500 XP cumulative, leaving 3,800 XP. Rank 20 itself costs 52,500 XP cumulative and therefore remains just beyond this exact horizon unless the Character has received slightly more than the Standard average or spent fewer resources elsewhere.

This demonstrates that 20-rank Skills are genuinely long-form mastery tracks rather than casual veteran purchases.

### 200-session Character — 101,300 total XP

Raising all six core Attributes from 10 to 20 costs 93,000 XP before Species modifiers or free creation allocation are considered, leaving only 8,300 XP from the synthetic all-XP-to-Attributes budget.

A Character can become extremely broad after a very long Campaign, but doing so requires major opportunity costs. CAB-07/08 simultaneous-power controls remain more appropriate than a blanket breadth tax.

## 6. Threshold timing at Standard pace

Assuming every earned XP point is reserved for the target and ignoring prerequisite/learning time:

- all higher-tier access T2→T5 in one free starting tree: 9,250 XP total; after the 1,300 start, about **15.9 Standard sessions** of earned XP;
- open an extra ordinary progression and buy access through T5: 10,250 XP; about **17.9 Standard sessions** after the starting grant;
- one Attribute 10→20: 15,500 XP; about **28.4 Standard sessions** after the starting grant;
- one Skill rank 20: 52,500 XP; about **102.4 Standard sessions** after the starting grant;
- all six Attributes 10→20: 93,000 XP; about **183.4 Standard sessions** after the starting grant.

These are lower-bound purchase-time figures. Real progression may take longer because individual Abilities, prerequisites, acquisition events and learning projects also consume time or XP.

## 7. Prestige simulation

Prestige remains an advanced acquisition mode, not a back-paid ordinary tree.

When explicit Prestige entry requirements are satisfied:

- Tier-3 access anchor: 1,250 XP;
- no T1/T2 back-pay;
- no automatically stacked 1,000-XP ordinary extra-progression opening fee;
- first Prestige Ability remains separately priced/granted.

At Standard pace, the access anchor is roughly 2.5 sessions of XP, but qualification requirements remain independently controlling. This makes Prestige entry expensive enough to be a meaningful commitment without turning its prerequisites into a hidden second currency.

## 8. Learning-speed simulation

The CAB-10 learning multiplier reaches its floor at relevant Attribute 20:

- Attribute 10: 1.00× learning time;
- 12: 0.90×;
- 14: 0.80×;
- 16: 0.70×;
- 18: 0.60×;
- 20+: 0.50× floor.

The ceiling prevents long-Campaign Intelligence/Wisdom from compounding into unlimited learning acceleration. It changes readiness time, not XP earned or XP price.

## 9. Equal-XP and RAV simulation posture

Equal paid XP comparisons remain useful only when RAV and dependencies are shown separately.

A Species-granted or milestone-granted capability may have 0 XP debit but meaningful RAV. That does not make the Character owe XP and does not justify stripping the grant. Instead, CAB-14/15 benchmarking shows the free capability as part of the Character's multidimensional profile and evaluates whether Campaign/Species packages create recurring outliers.

## 10. High-risk simulation boundary

The numerical progression model cannot by itself validate a high-risk Ability whose Action Economy, resource loop, stacking semantics, companion autonomy, immunity coverage or source text is unresolved.

Therefore:

- an 8,000+ XP price does not make an unbounded mechanic safe;
- a low XP price does not make an extra-Action or immunity mechanic acceptable;
- CAB-13 manual-review flags override simple budget comparison;
- CAB-22 must route these rows into explicit repair/repricing queues.

## 11. CAB-21 conclusions

1. **Retain 1,300 XP as the normal starting advancement grant.** The free creation chassis means it provides one substantial discretionary direction without trivializing advanced depth.
2. **Retain 500 XP per substantive session as the Standard long-run pacing target.** Slow/Fast/Accelerated profiles remain valid Campaign options at 250/750/1,000.
3. Keep Campaign awards accomplishment-based rather than forcing the pacing target into an automatic session stipend.
4. Retain current progression access, Attribute, Skill, Knowledge and learning anchors.
5. Retain direct HP only through the currently governed +20 ceiling until survivability testing supplies evidence for further blocks or repricing.
6. Do not introduce breadth taxes or readied slots; veteran budgets still impose meaningful opportunity costs while CAB-07/08 control nonlinear simultaneous power.
7. Treat all reference builds as synthetic QA fixtures, not canonical Character content.

CAB-21 therefore validates the integrated economic/pacing architecture strongly enough to proceed to final rules and content-repair mapping.
