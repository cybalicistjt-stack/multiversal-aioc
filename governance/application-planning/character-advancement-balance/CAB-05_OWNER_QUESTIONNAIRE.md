# CAB-05 — Owner Questionnaire

**Program:** CAB — Character Advancement & Balance  
**Purpose:** resolve the four five-tier policy choices that materially affect CAB-06 cost calibration. Unanswered questions remain unresolved and do not silently default.

## CAB-Q05-01 — Should the five numeric tiers have shared semantic descriptors?

**Decision impact:** MEDIUM  
**CAB recommendation:** **A**.

A. **Recommended:** keep the numeric tier authoritative and use a neutral shared descriptor set: **Tier 1 Foundation, Tier 2 Developed, Tier 3 Advanced, Tier 4 Expert, Tier 5 Apex**. Source-local names such as Legendary, Mastery, Elite, Basic, etc. remain valid display/provenance labels.

B. Use only Tier 1–5 numerically; define no shared descriptor words.

C. Use only source-local tier names; do not define cross-tree semantic descriptors.

D. Use another descriptor set you specify.

**Reasoning:** source tier names vary widely, while the numeric five-tier structure is strongly represented. A neutral descriptor vocabulary makes the depth model explainable without overwriting original labels.

## CAB-Q05-02 — Should ordinary purchasable Ability Trees normally charge a separate XP cost to unlock higher tiers?

**Decision impact:** VERY HIGH  
**CAB recommendation:** **A**.

A. **Recommended:** yes. For an ordinary purchasable tiered Ability progression, Tier 1 access comes from legitimately opening the progression; Tiers 2–5 normally require a **one-time tier-access XP purchase** after non-cost conditions are satisfied. Individual Abilities still have their own XP prices. CAB-06 calibrates the tier-access prices. Special progressions may explicitly use prerequisite-only, grant, milestone, sequential, innate, acquisition, or other access modes instead.

B. No separate tier-access XP cost. Once prerequisites are satisfied, only the individual Ability price is paid.

C. Do not define a CAB default; each Campaign/rules profile chooses whether ordinary trees use tier-access costs.

D. Defer the decision into CAB-06 while it models candidate price schedules.

**Reasoning:** separate tier access creates a visible cost for depth/specialization without restoring filler-buying quantity gates. The source corpus strongly attests tier-unlock costs, but not universally, so explicit special progression modes must remain possible.

## CAB-Q05-03 — How should short or sparse Ability Trees work?

**Decision impact:** HIGH  
**CAB recommendation:** **A**.

A. **Recommended:** a tree may intentionally cap before Tier 5. A well-formed tree should normally use contiguous tiers from its declared starting tier through its cap. Missing intermediate tiers are content-quality/provenance warnings unless explicitly intentional. Never invent filler Abilities merely to populate all five tiers.

B. Every ordinary Ability Tree must contain all five tiers; incomplete trees must eventually be expanded to five tiers.

C. Allow arbitrary sparse trees and skipped tiers without treating gaps as a warning.

D. Defer final policy to CAB-11/12 corpus and tree audits.

**Reasoning:** the bounded corpus contains 21 partial/sparse tiered groups. Requiring all five can force filler; allowing arbitrary unexplained gaps can hide damaged or incomplete source data. Option A distinguishes an intentional short tree from a content defect.

## CAB-Q05-04 — What does Tier 5 mean across different trees?

**Decision impact:** VERY HIGH  
**CAB recommendation:** **A**.

A. **Recommended:** Tier 5 means **Apex/capstone developmental depth within the owning progression**. It does not impose a universal "legendary," world-shaping, damage, price, or encounter-power floor across all domains. Actual effect burden and balance remain separately calibrated.

B. Tier 5 should have a universal legendary/world-shaping minimum regardless of tree or domain.

C. Tier 5 should impose a universal high-power floor, but each domain may define what that floor means.

D. Defer to CAB-15 multidimensional balance.

**Reasoning:** Tier-5 source records range from narrow combat techniques to major transformations and very high-cost psionics. A universal power floor would either overinflate narrow trees or misclassify broad effects. Tier can remain useful as depth without pretending all Tier-5 options are peers in raw power.

## Already established and not being re-asked

- AP is deprecated.
- Five tiers remain the working model.
- There is no universal prior-tier quantity ladder.
- XP, eligibility, prerequisites, tier access, learning, exclusions, approval, and commit are separate gates.
- Tier is not automatically XP price.
- Tree/path-specific explicit prerequisites and mastery counts remain legal.
- Free-product access to the first two tiers is entitlement, not balance.
- CAB-06 owns numeric prices.
