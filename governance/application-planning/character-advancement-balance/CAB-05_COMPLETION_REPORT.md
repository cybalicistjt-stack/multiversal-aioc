## Verified findings

- 4,816 bounded Ability records were examined.
- 3,917 carry numeric Tier 1–5.
- 179 tiered progression groups were observed.
- 158 contain all five tiers; 21 are partial/sparse/alternate-start.
- Direct XP medians rise by tier, but ranges overlap substantially, so tier cannot serve as an exact power or price scalar.
- Source-local tier names vary and remain provenance/display labels.

## Owner-approved tier rules

1. Numeric Tier 1–5 remains authoritative.
2. Shared descriptors are **Foundation / Developed / Advanced / Expert / Apex**.
3. Tier means relative developmental depth within the owning progression scope.
4. Ordinary purchasable progressions normally use a one-time XP access purchase for Tiers 2–5 after non-cost requirements are satisfied; individual Ability prices remain separate.
5. Special progressions may explicitly use prerequisite-only, grant, milestone, sequential, innate, special-acquisition, or other governed access modes.
6. There is no universal prior-tier quantity ladder.
7. Ordinary short progressions may intentionally cap before Tier 5; contiguous tiers from declared start through cap are expected, unexplained internal gaps are warnings, and filler is never invented.
8. **Prestige classes/progressions may legitimately start at Tier 3 with explicit entry requirements.** Tiers 1–2 are not missing in that declared Prestige scope.
9. Tier 5 means Apex/capstone depth inside the owning progression and does not impose a universal world-shaping or raw-power floor.
10. Product/free-user tier access remains entitlement, not balance semantics.
