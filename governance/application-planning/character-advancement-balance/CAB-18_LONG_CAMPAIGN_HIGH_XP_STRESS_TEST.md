# CAB-18 — Long-Campaign / High-XP Stress Test

## Purpose

Stress the CAB economy at veteran/high-XP horizons to determine whether the system needs global breadth taxes, diminishing returns, hidden caps, or other controls beyond the established price, prerequisite, action, stacking, resource and acquisition rules.

## Standard-campaign horizons

At the CAB-16 Standard target of 500 earned XP per substantive session plus the 1,300 normal start:

- 24 sessions → ~13,300 total advancement XP;
- 50 sessions → ~26,300;
- 100 sessions → ~51,300;
- 200 sessions → ~101,300.

Actual campaigns vary around these figures; they are stress checkpoints.

## Cost concentration checks

Under CAB-10/CAB-06:

- one Attribute 10→20 costs 15,500 XP;
- all six Attributes 10→20 would cost 93,000 XP before other advancement;
- one Skill 0→20 costs 52,500 XP;
- one Knowledge field to Pioneer costs 7,750 XP;
- opening an extra ordinary progression and buying T2–T5 access costs 10,250 XP before individual Ability purchases;
- high-risk Abilities/bundles may require 8,000+ direct XP and manual review.

Therefore 50k–100k XP produces a powerful veteran but does not cheaply maximize every Attribute, Skill, Knowledge and progression simultaneously.

## Breadth stress

At high XP a Character can own many low/mid-cost options. CAB deliberately permits this. The stress question is whether those options can all matter simultaneously.

CAB-07/08 controls remain the main constraints:

- one default Action + Bonus Action + Reaction envelope;
- action multiplier anti-recursion;
- meaningful Free/No-Action limiters;
- same-group stacking restrictions;
- strongest-only same-group multipliers;
- companion action-economy accounting;
- Resources/frequency/duration/dependencies.

A veteran's large library is therefore not itself evidence for diminishing-return purchase prices or readied slots.

## Concentration stress

A specialist can spend heavily in one Attribute, Skill, tree, defensive package, magic capacity or companion package. CAB-15 treats strong specialization as legitimate. The warning state is simultaneous dominance across several dimensions without corresponding opportunity cost/counterplay.

High-risk multiplier purchases remain the primary path to nonlinear growth; CAB-13 manual review must therefore become stricter, not looser, at veteran budgets.

## INT/WIS divergence

CAB-10 learning acceleration caps at 50% of baseline time and never creates extra spendable XP. A high-INT/WIS veteran may complete applicable learning projects faster, but cannot compound that advantage into a permanent percentage XP-income lead. The cap prevents exponential long-campaign divergence.

## RAV/grant stress

Long campaigns may accumulate free milestone, Species, faction, relic, transformation or other grants. RAV must remain visible. A zero-debit grant does not create XP debt, but repeated high-RAV grants can materially alter a campaign's power assumptions and should be included in CAB-14/15 benchmark comparisons.

## Stress-test conclusions

The current CAB architecture does **not** justify:

- a global breadth tax;
- global diminishing-return Ability prices;
- known-Ability caps;
- readied slots imposed on all Ability families;
- universal species/environment caps;
- Track XP gates;
- global XP multipliers for catch-up or Intelligence.

It does require:

- high-risk manual review and repricing;
- strong action/stacking/resource semantics;
- explicit acquisition/dependency state;
- RAV visibility;
- periodic benchmark/playtest calibration at veteran budgets.

## Adopted decisions

Under standing owner delegation, CAB-18 confirms the existing no-breadth-tax architecture and routes nonlinear veteran risk to simultaneous-power/high-risk controls instead. No delegation guardrail triggered.

**Successor:** CAB-19 — Respec, Correction & Migration.
