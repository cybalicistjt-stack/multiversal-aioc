# CEW-15 — Monster, Extraordinary Creature & Creature-Type Gap Expansion Contract

**Contract:** `CEW-MON-EXTRA-1.0`  
**Work item:** CEW-15 — Monster, Extraordinary Creature & Creature-Type Gap Expansion  
**Authority:** content/recovery/design/provenance only; no application implementation authority.

## Purpose

CEW-15 closes the extraordinary-creature and creature-type coverage gaps left after source recovery, ordinary wildlife expansion, and alien-wildlife expansion. Source evidence remains authoritative for recovered taxonomy usage; first-party profiles remain noncanonical design material.

## Retained extraordinary source corpus

CEW-15 accounts for 19 retained extraordinary/monster source documents containing 741 safe statblock records. This is source-coverage accounting, not promotion. Source records do not become canonical Creature Definitions merely because they have complete statblocks, familiar names, matching mechanics, or recognizable types.

## Creature-type gap resolutions

### Beast

The retained `Beast Creatures 1.PDF` and `Beasts 2.PDF` repeatedly use Beast as a base Type label across 199 safe statblock records. This supports a bounded Beast source-usage contract.

**Beast does not become synonymous with Animal.** The source label does not itself establish ecology, cognition, personhood, domestication, relationship eligibility, native range, or canonical identity.

### Illusion

`Incorporeal Creatures.PDF` contains four explicit `Type: Illusion` records. Therefore **Illusion is source-supported as a Type value**. `Mirage` is observed as a source subtype value. Neither label creates canonical identity, personhood, distribution, or encounter placement.

### Dragon

`Dragons(1).PDF` names five major dragon categories: Reptilian Dragons, Arcane-Born Dragons, Chromatic Dragons, Bird-Dragons, and Exo Dragons. It also provides a common stage reference of Hatchling, Wyrmling, Juvenile, Adult, Elder, Ancient, and Greatwyrm while explicitly allowing category/family-specific alternatives.

Accordingly there is **no universal dragon stage ladder**. Category/family-specific stage authority is preserved, including simpler Young/Adult handling where the source uses it.

### Canonical stable-ID bindings

The 27 current canonical Creature Definitions still lack explicit stable-ID type-binding authority. **canonical stable-ID type bindings remain unknown**. CEW-15 creates zero type bindings by name, mechanics, collection membership, habitat resemblance, or source similarity.

## Extraordinary environment handoff

CEW-13 deferred three extraordinary environments to CEW-15: Volcano, Post-Apocalyptic Radioactive Zone, and Ashland. CEW-15 authors six governed first-party noncanonical extraordinary profiles across those contexts.

These profiles are ecological/design material only. **environment suitability does not create canonical distribution**. Environment selection does not create encounter placement, native status, canonical identity, relationship state, or a live creature instance.

## First-party extraordinary profile boundary

Each CEW-15 profile:
- has no canonical Creature Definition binding;
- has no canonical World/Reality/Setting/Place distribution binding;
- creates no statblock;
- leaves personhood unknown unless separately governed;
- creates no mount, pet, familiar, companion, ownership, taming, training, or bond state;
- creates no encounter or campaign placement;
- remains explicitly provenance-marked as governed first-party CEW-15 design material.

No species/monster quota or numeric ecology/gap score is authorized.

## Mutation boundary

CEW-15 authorizes no `Multiversal-app` runtime, schema, UI, migration, canonical Creature Definition, canonical distribution, live relationship, or encounter-placement mutation.

## Handoff

The strict successor is **CEW-16 — GM Environment Discovery, Encounter Ecology & Full Integration Handoff**.

CEW-16 may consume the completed CEW-01..15 contracts and libraries as read-only governed content/projection authority. It must preserve identity, distribution, personhood, relationship, and placement boundaries rather than flattening them into environment similarity.
