## 1. Farmland / Agricultural Countryside
### Overview
Cultivated rural land shaped by fields, orchards, pasture, irrigation, fences, farm lanes and dispersed buildings. It is productive open country rather than wilderness or a dense settlement.
### Environmental features
- Field blocks, hedgerows, ditches, terraces, barns, silos, wells and livestock enclosures.
- Seasonal crop height and field condition can change sightlines and traversability.
- Water access and soil condition strongly affect useful routes and resources.
### Movement and navigation
- Farm lanes and field edges provide easy routes until gates, fences, ditches or soft ground interrupt them.
- Tall crops, orchards and hedge networks can create local concealment and maze-like navigation.
- Cross-country travel may damage crops or attract attention even when physically easy.
### Hazards
- Irrigation ditches, unstable hay or grain storage, farm machinery, animal enclosures and contaminated water are common site hazards.
- Mud, dust, fire risk and crop failure are conditions, not universal baseline overlays.
### Encounters and challenges
- Access disputes, damaged infrastructure, lost livestock, theft, crop disease, water allocation and harvest timing create noncombat challenges.
- Farmsteads may provide food, information, tools and temporary shelter if relations allow.
### Rest and shelter
Barns, sheds, windbreaks, farmhouses, abandoned outbuildings and field camps may provide shelter. Availability depends on ownership, occupancy and local conditions.
### Random encounters (d12)
1. A blocked irrigation gate backs water across a field.
2. Workers search for a missing animal near a hedgerow.
3. A wagon or cart has broken an axle on a farm lane.
4. A field fire has just started at the edge of dry stubble.
5. A boundary dispute halts travel through a gate.
6. A collapsed shed exposes stored tools and unstable debris.
7. A dense crop stand conceals an unexpected obstacle.
8. A well or pump has stopped working during a critical task.
9. A harvest crew urgently needs extra hands.
10. A fenced pasture forces a detour around agitated livestock.
11. A drainage ditch has washed out a crossing.
12. A remote farmstead signals for assistance.

## 2. Suburb / Residential District
### Overview
Low- to medium-density permanent residential development with homes, local streets, yards, small commercial or civic sites and utility networks. It sits between settlement-scale and fully urban structure.
### Environmental features
- Repeating streets, cul-de-sacs, yards, alleys, drainage systems, parks and utility corridors.
- Detached or clustered buildings provide many small interiors rather than a few large complexes.
- Local schools, shops, clinics, community buildings and service nodes may punctuate residential areas.
### Movement and navigation
- Street grids are usually legible but cul-de-sacs, walls, canals and private compounds can break direct travel.
- Backyards, alleys, pedestrian paths and drainage corridors create alternate routes.
- Vehicle access depends on road condition and obstruction; vehicle mechanics remain externally owned.
### Hazards
- Damaged utilities, house fires, structural failure, traffic hazards, unsecured pools or pits and localized contamination may occur.
- Blackouts, flooding and infrastructure collapse are later overlay conditions, not automatic suburb traits.
### Encounters and challenges
- Neighborhood disputes, evacuations, missing residents, blocked services, looting, local patrols or mutual-aid efforts may shape play.
- Dense private property creates information and access challenges distinct from open countryside.
### Rest and shelter
Homes, garages, community buildings, basements and sheltered yards may support rest if safe and accessible.
### Random encounters (d12)
1. A fallen tree blocks the only through street.
2. Residents organize an improvised checkpoint.
3. A utility crew is investigating a dangerous service failure.
4. An unattended alarm sounds from a nearby house.
5. A drainage channel offers a concealed shortcut.
6. A local gathering has closed a small intersection.
7. A damaged roof threatens to collapse into a yard.
8. A neighborhood animal has escaped its enclosure.
9. A parked vehicle blocks a narrow access lane.
10. A community building has become an informal refuge.
11. Someone signals from an upper-story window.
12. A shortcut through connected yards provokes an access dispute.

## 3. Frontier Outpost
### Overview
A small remote settlement or station established to support trade, travel, exploration, defense or resource access at the edge of a larger network.
### Environmental features
- Compact habitation, storage, workshops, perimeter markers and one or more controlled access points.
- Limited supplies make wells, generators, stores, landing areas or corrals disproportionately important.
- The surrounding environment remains close and materially affects daily operation.
### Movement and navigation
- Internal movement is simple, but approach routes may be few and exposed.
- Gates, walls, ditches, watch points or terrain barriers may channel entry.
### Hazards
- Supply failure, water shortage, damaged communications, fire, local structural weakness and isolated medical emergencies are common risks.
- Extreme weather and infrastructure failure remain overlays when active.
### Encounters and challenges
- Trade disputes, overdue caravans, missing patrols, repair shortages, jurisdiction conflicts and unfamiliar travelers are common.
- The outpost can function as refuge, checkpoint, market, rumor hub or expedition staging site.
### Rest and shelter
Barracks, inns, warehouses, workshops and protected camp areas may provide reliable shelter if the outpost is functioning and access is permitted.
### Random encounters (d12)
1. A supply caravan is late and expected soon.
2. The main gate has jammed partly open.
3. A water ration dispute is escalating.
4. A damaged beacon or signal tower needs repair.
5. A traveler brings contradictory news from the next route.
6. A storage building has been broken into.
7. A perimeter watch reports movement outside the lights.
8. A local official seeks neutral witnesses for a dispute.
9. A workshop urgently needs a missing component.
10. A temporary camp has formed outside the official perimeter.
11. A returning patrol carries an injured member.
12. A departing expedition offers information in exchange for help.

## 4. Road / Wilderness Trail
### Overview
A linear terrestrial movement corridor connecting places across surrounding terrain. It may be paved, graded, packed earth, marked path or lightly maintained trail.
### Environmental features
- Route surface, shoulders, verges, cuttings, embankments, switchbacks, junctions, signs and waymarkers.
- Crossings, culverts, bridges, gates and passes create natural chokepoints.
- The corridor inherits surrounding terrain conditions without becoming that terrain.
### Movement and navigation
- Following the corridor usually improves direction-finding and travel efficiency.
- Detours become important when the route is blocked, washed out, collapsed or controlled.
- Junctions and parallel tracks create navigation decisions even when the path itself is obvious.
### Hazards
- Erosion, washouts, unstable shoulders, fallen debris, damaged crossings and blind corners are baseline route hazards.
- Floods, landslides, wildfire, snow closure and severe weather remain overlays.
### Encounters and challenges
- Broken transport, tolls, checkpoints, roadwork, travelers in distress, misleading signs and disputed crossings are common.
- Ambush potential comes from corridor predictability, not from a universal combat rule.
### Rest and shelter
Roadside shelters, trail huts, lay-bys, camps, culverts, abandoned structures and protected clearings may provide limited rest opportunities.
### Random encounters (d12)
1. A bridge or crossing is unsafe but not fully impassable.
2. Fresh tracks leave the route toward rough terrain.
3. A disabled cart or vehicle blocks most of the corridor.
4. A signpost points in two contradictory directions.
5. Road workers or travelers are clearing fallen debris.
6. A toll or inspection point has been established ahead.
7. A washed-out shoulder narrows the route to a risky passage.
8. A side trail appears recently used.
9. A stranded traveler requests assistance.
10. A milestone or marker has been deliberately altered.
11. A temporary camp occupies the safest stopping place.
12. The route ends at an unexpected closure requiring a detour.

## 5. Mine / Quarry
### Overview
A resource-extraction environment combining engineered access, excavation faces, spoil areas, hauling routes and potentially extensive underground workings.
### Environmental features
- Shafts, adits, pits, benches, tunnels, hoists, conveyors, stockpiles and support structures.
- Mines emphasize enclosed subterranean networks; quarries emphasize exposed cuts and steep artificial relief.
- Drainage, ventilation and structural support may be essential to safe operation.
### Movement and navigation
- Controlled haul routes and access tunnels create efficient movement when intact.
- Vertical shafts, ladders, lifts, ramps and narrow passages constrain alternatives.
- Excavation can produce confusing branching layouts and dead ends.
### Hazards
- Collapse, unstable faces, pits, dust, poor air, machinery, blasting remnants and flooded workings are common risks.
- Toxic atmosphere, contamination and active geologic disaster remain overlays unless specifically present.
### Encounters and challenges
- Trapped workers, lost survey routes, equipment failure, disputed claims, missing shipments and unsafe abandoned sections create challenges.
- Valuable material may attract guarded access or scavenging.
### Rest and shelter
Surface offices, workshops, bunkhouses, reinforced chambers and dry side passages may provide shelter; abandoned workings can be deceptively unsafe.
### Random encounters (d12)
1. A support beam has shifted and the passage creaks under load.
2. A lift or hoist is stopped between levels.
3. Fresh water is entering a lower working unexpectedly.
4. Survey markings disagree at a tunnel junction.
5. A blasting area has been left incompletely cleared.
6. A haul route is blocked by fallen material.
7. Workers refuse to enter a section after a recent incident.
8. A ventilation fan or duct has failed.
9. An abandoned side tunnel has been reopened recently.
10. A stockpile collapse has buried equipment.
11. A quarry ledge offers a shortcut with dangerous footing.
12. A missing crew is overdue from a remote working face.

## 6. Factory / Refinery
### Overview
A production or processing site organized around machinery, material flow, work cells, storage, control rooms and service systems.
### Environmental features
- Production halls, pipe racks, tanks, conveyors, loading areas, workshops, offices and maintenance corridors.
- Different process zones may have sharply different access, noise, heat and hazard profiles.
### Movement and navigation
- Marked walkways and service routes are efficient but may be interrupted by machinery, locked access or active process zones.
- Elevated catwalks, ladders, maintenance spaces and loading bays create multiple vertical routes.
### Hazards
- Moving machinery, pressure systems, heat, fumes, spills, energized equipment and confined spaces are common industrial risks.
- Major toxic release, firestorm or systems collapse remains an overlay/event state rather than universal baseline.
### Encounters and challenges
- Shutdown procedures, missing workers, sabotage, labor disputes, damaged production lines, guarded materials and emergency isolation can drive play.
### Rest and shelter
Offices, break rooms, workshops and shut-down sections may permit rest if the site is stable and access is safe.
### Random encounters (d12)
1. An emergency stop has halted one production line.
2. A leaking pipe forces a route change.
3. A locked control room contains the only useful system panel.
4. Workers are evacuating one section but not another.
5. A conveyor carries unattended material toward a jam.
6. A maintenance crew requests help isolating machinery.
7. A loud alarm masks ordinary communication.
8. A spill has made a floor dangerously slick.
9. A loading bay is blocked by damaged cargo.
10. A security inspection is underway at an internal checkpoint.
11. Power has failed in one wing while machinery elsewhere remains active.
12. An abandoned maintenance passage bypasses a controlled area.

## 7. Power Plant / Utility Complex
### Overview
Critical infrastructure that produces or distributes power, water, heat, communications or another essential service through tightly coupled engineered systems.
### Environmental features
- Generation or treatment areas, control rooms, substations, pumps, switchgear, reservoirs, utility tunnels and secured service yards.
- Redundancy and isolation zones may make the layout more compartmentalized than ordinary industry.
### Movement and navigation
- Authorized routes are clear, but locked technical zones, cable galleries and utility tunnels can create complex secondary paths.
- Some areas may require shutdown or isolation before safe passage.
### Hazards
- Electrical systems, pressure, rotating machinery, hot surfaces, treatment chemicals, deep water or stored energy may be present depending on facility type.
- Radiation or toxic contamination is not universal and remains condition/source specific.
### Encounters and challenges
- Load imbalance, partial blackout, damaged pumps, unauthorized access, emergency repair, competing restoration priorities and failing backups are common challenges.
### Rest and shelter
Administrative areas, workshops and isolated control spaces may provide strong shelter while operating plant zones remain unsuitable.
### Random encounters (d12)
1. A backup system starts unexpectedly.
2. A control panel reports conflicting sensor readings.
3. A maintenance hatch has been left open.
4. A local outage has brought residents or officials to the gate.
5. A pump or generator is vibrating beyond normal limits.
6. A service tunnel is partly flooded.
7. Security seals show recent tampering.
8. A technician is missing during an emergency check.
9. An automated isolation door closes across the route.
10. A supply of critical replacement parts is nearly exhausted.
11. Two crews disagree about which system must be restored first.
12. A dormant subsystem can provide an alternate route if reactivated.

## 8. Fortress / Military Base
### Overview
A deliberately fortified complex organized around controlled access, defense, command, storage, training and sustained occupancy.
### Environmental features
- Walls, gates, towers, bunkers, barracks, magazines, motor pools, parade or training grounds and command structures.
- Sightlines and routes are intentionally shaped by security and defense needs.
### Movement and navigation
- Gates, checkpoints, walls and layered internal zones constrain access.
- Service tunnels, maintenance routes and defensive passages may provide alternate movement.
### Hazards
- Fortifications, restricted zones, stored munitions, defensive structures and damaged barriers create risks.
- Active bombardment, siege, fire or contamination are event/overlay conditions, not universal baseline.
### Encounters and challenges
- Inspections, changing orders, command disputes, prisoners, supply shortages, drills, damaged defenses and uncertain allegiance can shape play.
### Rest and shelter
Barracks, bunkers, protected courtyards, command posts and hardened storage provide strong shelter when accessible.
### Random encounters (d12)
1. A gate inspection becomes unusually thorough.
2. A training exercise blocks a major internal route.
3. A sentry reports a missing authorization record.
4. A supply detail has misplaced important equipment.
5. A defensive position is found unexpectedly unmanned.
6. A restricted corridor has been opened for repairs.
7. A command dispute delays a necessary decision.
8. A damaged wall or fence creates an unplanned access point.
9. A prisoner transfer requires additional guards or witnesses.
10. A magazine or armory alarm triggers a lockdown.
11. A patrol returns early with conflicting reports.
12. A hardened shelter is being reassigned during an emergency.

## 9. Transit Hub / Terminal
### Overview
A transport interchange where multiple routes, schedules, passengers, cargo and support systems converge. Variants include rail terminals, bus depots, airports, spaceports and intermodal hubs.
### Environmental features
- Platforms, gates, concourses, cargo areas, service roads, maintenance zones, ticketing or control areas and secure operational spaces.
- Public circulation overlays technical back-of-house infrastructure.
### Movement and navigation
- Numbered gates, platforms and concourses support rapid navigation when systems are functioning.
- Security barriers, schedule changes, closed platforms and baggage/cargo systems can redirect movement.
### Hazards
- Moving vehicles, platform edges, fuel or power systems, heavy cargo, crowd pressure and restricted operational zones are common.
- Storm closure, blackout or systems failure remains a later overlay/event.
### Encounters and challenges
- Delays, lost cargo, missing passengers, security alerts, schedule conflicts, labor actions, customs problems and rerouting are common.
### Rest and shelter
Waiting areas, lounges, offices, maintenance rooms and terminals can provide excellent shelter when open; operational zones are usually unsuitable.
### Random encounters (d12)
1. A departure gate changes with little warning.
2. Unclaimed cargo triggers a security response.
3. A platform or concourse is abruptly closed.
4. A maintenance vehicle blocks a service route.
5. A traveler has lost essential documents.
6. A system outage disables signs in one section.
7. A crowd forms around a delayed departure.
8. Staff begin rerouting traffic through a normally restricted passage.
9. A baggage or cargo system jams.
10. A security checkpoint receives conflicting instructions.
11. An arriving service carries an unexpected emergency case.
12. A last-minute connection requires crossing the entire hub quickly.

## 10. Harbor / Dockyards
### Overview
An active waterfront logistics and vessel-support environment focused on berthing, loading, repair, storage and transfer between land and water. It is distinct from the broader civic identity of a Port City.
### Environmental features
- Piers, wharves, slips, cranes, warehouses, dry docks, shipyards, breakwaters and service yards.
- Water level and shoreline geometry affect access to vessels and low structures.
### Movement and navigation
- Narrow piers, gangways, ladders and dock roads create constrained routes with frequent edges and crossings.
- Cargo stacks and active loading operations can block direct movement.
### Hazards
- Deep water, slippery surfaces, suspended loads, moving vessels, cables, fuel, machinery and unstable dock edges are common.
- Storm surge, flood, severe weather and contamination remain overlays when active.
### Encounters and challenges
- Delayed cargo, berth disputes, damaged vessels, customs inspections, missing workers, salvage claims and loading accidents create challenges.
### Rest and shelter
Warehouses, offices, workshops, vessel interiors and covered sheds may provide shelter if access and safety permit.
### Random encounters (d12)
1. A mooring line parts and a vessel begins drifting from its berth.
2. A cargo crane stops with a load suspended overhead.
3. A customs or inspection team seals one warehouse.
4. A damaged gangway leaves a vessel difficult to reach.
5. A worker falls or drops equipment near the waterline.
6. A berth assignment dispute blocks a pier.
7. A tug or service craft needs emergency assistance.
8. A leaking container is isolated from nearby cargo.
9. A dry dock gate will not cycle normally.
10. A manifest does not match the cargo being unloaded.
11. A narrow service route is blocked by stacked freight.
12. An incoming vessel signals a problem before docking.
