# ENV-08 — Grasslands, Open Country & Dry Landforms Preset Content

**Program:** ENV — Environment Preset & Overlay  
**Tranche:** ENV-08 — Grasslands, Open Country & Dry Landforms  
**Authority class:** owner-authorized new content, not recovered source text  
**Application implementation authority:** none

## Scope and authorship boundary

This package adds eight GM-ready presets using the established ENV composition model. It does not rewrite the forty retained source profiles and does not create new executable overlays. Each preset supplies overview, environmental features, movement/navigation, hazards, encounters/challenges, rest/shelter, and a d12 random encounter table.

The encounter material uses ecological roles, signs, terrain events and generic travelers rather than declaring canonical creature identities or world distributions. CEW owns those mappings. Habitat Signature vocabulary remains ENV-15. Extreme heat, drought, wildfire, sandstorm/dust storm, extreme cold and supernatural conditions remain ENV-11/12/13 overlays or local/source facts rather than being silently baked into every dry/open preset.

## 1. Grassland / Prairie

**Preset:** `ENVPRESET-OC-GRASSLAND`  
**Primary archetype:** `ARCH-OPEN-COUNTRY`

### Overview

Broad grass-dominated country with long sightlines, few fixed obstacles and shelter concentrated around folds in the land, waterways, isolated trees, rock or human-made structures. Grass height, soil, moisture and seasonal growth are local parameters rather than separate environment identities.

### Environmental features

- broad grass cover with patches of bare soil, flowers, low brush or exposed rock;
- shallow rises, swales, drainage lines and occasional isolated trees or copses;
- long horizons and highly visible movement;
- game trails, grazing signs, burrows and seasonal water points;
- firebreaks, fences, tracks or field edges where settlements are nearby.

### Movement and navigation

- generally efficient overland travel where ground is firm;
- long sightlines aid orientation but can make stealth and concealment difficult;
- tall grass can hide holes, low obstacles, trails and small hazards;
- wind, rain, mud or snow effects require local facts or later overlays;
- mounted or vehicle movement is evaluated by their owning systems rather than defined here.

### Hazards

- exposure with little natural shelter;
- hidden holes, burrows, washouts or low obstacles;
- tall dry vegetation creating fire potential without assuming an active wildfire;
- limited water away from streams, ponds or settlements;
- lightning, severe storms and temperature extremes require later weather/physical authority.

### Encounters and challenges

Use migrating or grazing wildlife signs, distant movement, lost trails, water access, controlled burns, fences, isolated camps, exposed crossings, changing vegetation height and decisions between speed and concealment.

### Rest and shelter

Natural shelter is limited. Depressions, stream banks, isolated groves, rock shelves, hay or field structures and nearby settlements can provide cover. A camp on an exposed crest is visible and vulnerable to changing weather.

### Random encounters (d12)

1. A broad animal trail crosses the route toward distant water.
2. Tall grass conceals a shallow wash or hole.
3. A lone tree or rock outcrop offers the only nearby shade and is already in use.
4. Distant movement is visible on the horizon but difficult to identify.
5. A fence, boundary marker or old field line interrupts the straight route.
6. Fresh grazing or feeding signs cover a large patch of ground.
7. A low swale holds unexpected water, mud or lush growth.
8. Wind lays the grass flat in one direction, revealing a hidden path.
9. Smoke is visible far away without revealing whether it is camp, settlement or fire.
10. A burrowed area creates unstable footing across the preferred line of travel.
11. A traveler or work party is using the same exposed route.
12. The shortest path crosses open high ground while a longer route follows concealed low terrain.

## 2. Savanna

**Preset:** `ENVPRESET-OC-SAVANNA`  
**Primary archetype:** `ARCH-OPEN-COUNTRY`

### Overview

Open grassland interspersed with scattered trees, shrubs and seasonal concentrations of water. The defining structure remains open country; tree density is too discontinuous to become Forest unless a local instance actually transitions into woodland.

### Environmental features

- grass-dominated ground with scattered trees or tree clusters;
- seasonal water holes, drainage channels and dry streambeds;
- broad visibility interrupted by isolated vegetation;
- shade and browse concentrated around trees and water;
- burned, grazed or freshly grown patches depending on local history.

### Movement and navigation

- open stretches permit efficient travel and long-range orientation;
- isolated trees and drainage lines create landmarks and route choices;
- thorny brush or dense grass can locally slow travel;
- water availability may shape routes more than terrain difficulty;
- active drought, monsoon or wildfire states remain overlays/local facts.

### Hazards

- heat and exposure where locally applicable, but extreme heat is not baseline authority;
- scarce dependable water in dry periods;
- thorny vegetation, hidden holes and dry channels;
- concentration of large wildlife around water or shade without naming canonical creatures;
- active brushfire or severe weather requires later overlay authority.

### Encounters and challenges

Use water-hole access, grazing pressure, herd movement signs, predator/prey evidence, thorn thickets, abandoned camps, burned patches, distant storms and competition for shade or water.

### Rest and shelter

Large trees, rock outcrops, dry channels and built structures can offer shade or windbreak. Water holes may be valuable rest sites but can also concentrate wildlife and other travelers.

### Random encounters (d12)

1. A heavily used path converges on a distant water hole.
2. Scattered trees suddenly give way to a dense thorny patch.
3. Fresh tracks and trampled grass indicate a large group passed recently.
4. A shaded tree shows signs of repeated resting activity.
5. A dry channel offers cover but poor visibility around its bends.
6. A recently burned patch creates easy travel and little concealment.
7. Birds or other small fauna gather above a point beyond the next rise.
8. A shallow muddy water source is usable but heavily contested by signs of traffic.
9. Distant dust reveals movement across the open ground.
10. A fallen tree or brush pile forms an unexpected local barrier and shelter point.
11. Travelers are rationing water and ask about the nearest reliable source.
12. The direct route crosses exposed grass while a longer line links scattered shade and cover.

## 3. Steppe

**Preset:** `ENVPRESET-OC-STEPPE`  
**Primary archetype:** `ARCH-OPEN-COUNTRY`

### Overview

Dry to semi-arid open grass and shrub country, often expansive, windswept and sparsely treed. Steppe emphasizes distance, exposure, limited surface water and repeated low vegetation rather than the dunes of Sandy Desert or broken rock of Rocky Desert.

### Environmental features

- low grasses, herbs and shrubs with broad uninterrupted horizons;
- shallow gullies, dry drainage lines and occasional rocky patches;
- sparse tree cover;
- wind-shaped vegetation and exposed soils;
- isolated springs, streams or seasonal water points where locally present.

### Movement and navigation

- broad visibility supports long-distance route planning;
- few major obstacles can make distance deceptive;
- gullies and low rises may remain unseen until close;
- firm dry ground usually supports straightforward travel;
- dust storms, snow, extreme cold/heat and drought remain later overlays or local facts.

### Hazards

- prolonged exposure with little shelter;
- uncertain water between known sources;
- wind and blowing dust under ordinary conditions without assuming a dust-storm overlay;
- hidden gullies, loose soil and burrows;
- rapid weather change on open ground where locally established.

### Encounters and challenges

Use nomadic or migratory routes, old camp rings, wells, wind-scoured markers, wildlife tracks, distant riders/travelers, abandoned wagons or loads, and route decisions between known water and a shorter uncertain path.

### Rest and shelter

Low ground, cut banks, rock shelves, tents, vehicles or settlements are often more valuable than natural tree cover. Wind direction can determine whether a shallow depression is useful shelter or a dust trap.

### Random encounters (d12)

1. A line of old markers points toward a water source not shown on the current route.
2. Wind exposes a buried track or old campsite.
3. A shallow gully provides sudden cover from the open horizon.
4. Distant travelers alter course after noticing the party.
5. Animal tracks converge on a sparse spring or seep.
6. A patch of loose soil or burrows slows otherwise easy travel.
7. A broken cart, load or abandoned camp lies far from obvious settlement.
8. The horizon appears closer than it is, adding unexpected travel time.
9. A low ridge hides a broad basin until the group reaches the crest.
10. Dust hangs over a distant moving group or herd.
11. A known water point is usable but reduced or fouled by ordinary causes.
12. The route divides between a direct exposed line and a longer sheltered gully system.

## 4. Scrubland / Chaparral

**Preset:** `ENVPRESET-OC-SCRUB`  
**Primary archetype:** `ARCH-OPEN-COUNTRY`

### Overview

Open country dominated by dense low shrubs, brush and hardy vegetation. Sightlines are more broken than prairie or steppe, but the vegetation remains primarily low and discontinuous rather than creating a tree-canopy Forest baseline.

### Environmental features

- thorny, woody or resinous shrubs with irregular gaps;
- exposed rock, dry soil and narrow animal or human paths;
- gullies, low ridges and drainage channels;
- pockets of denser brush that can hide movement at short range;
- burn scars or regrowth mosaics where local history supports them.

### Movement and navigation

- travel is easiest along established paths, ridgelines and natural gaps;
- forcing through dense scrub can slow movement and increase noise;
- brush can conceal low obstacles and route changes;
- navigation may alternate between broad views from rises and poor visibility within thickets;
- wildfire and drought states remain overlays rather than baseline assumptions.

### Hazards

- thorny or abrasive vegetation;
- snagging equipment and restricted movement in dense patches;
- hidden holes, loose rock and dry channels;
- limited shade and water in dry instances;
- high fire potential where vegetation is dry, distinct from an active Wildfire overlay.

### Encounters and challenges

Use concealed trails, territorial signs, sudden clearings, abandoned shelters, brush-choked ruins, wildlife movement through narrow corridors, difficult access to water, and route choices between exposed ridges and obstructed thickets.

### Rest and shelter

Rock overhangs, larger shrubs, gullies, cleared ground and built structures may offer windbreak and concealment. Dense dry brush is poor camp placement when fire risk is relevant.

### Random encounters (d12)

1. A narrow trail disappears into a dense thicket.
2. Thorny brush snags gear along the obvious shortcut.
3. A hidden clearing contains signs of recent use.
4. Movement in nearby scrub is audible before it is visible.
5. A shallow gully cuts across the route and offers concealed travel.
6. A patch of old burn scar is easier to cross than surrounding vegetation.
7. A rock outcrop provides a rare elevated view over the brush.
8. Fresh tracks follow the same narrow path in both directions.
9. A small water source is concealed beneath dense vegetation.
10. Brush has grown over a former road, wall or structure.
11. A traveler emerges unexpectedly from a parallel concealed route.
12. The party must choose between slow cover through scrub and fast exposure across open ground.

## 5. Hills / Uplands

**Preset:** `ENVPRESET-DL-HILLS`  
**Primary archetype:** `ARCH-HIGHLAND`  
**Secondary archetype:** `ARCH-OPEN-COUNTRY`

### Overview

Rolling to strongly broken elevated terrain where repeated climbs, descents, ridgelines, saddles and valleys matter more than extreme mountain verticality. Open uplands combine long views with route costs hidden by relief.

### Environmental features

- ridges, slopes, saddles, shallow valleys and isolated higher points;
- mixed soil, grass, scrub and rock depending on local biome;
- stream cuts and gullies descending toward lower ground;
- exposed crests and sheltered lee slopes;
- repeated changes in line of sight.

### Movement and navigation

- straight-line distance understates travel cost because of repeated elevation change;
- ridgelines improve orientation but increase exposure;
- valleys and saddles often provide easier routes but may limit visibility;
- slopes can become difficult when wet, icy or loose, with those states supplied externally;
- exact altitude/low-oxygen effects remain ENV-12 where applicable.

### Hazards

- fatigue from repeated climbs and descents without defining character-system mechanics;
- slips, loose rock and short falls;
- hidden gullies and sudden slope breaks;
- exposure to wind or weather on crests;
- localized erosion or unstable ground.

### Encounters and challenges

Use ridge crossings, lost paths, hilltop ruins or markers, sheltered valleys, grazing or nesting signs, difficult stream cuts, travelers visible at distance and tactical choices between high visibility and low-route concealment.

### Rest and shelter

Lee slopes, shallow caves, rock walls, wooded pockets and valley bottoms may offer protection. Hilltops provide visibility but poor shelter.

### Random encounters (d12)

1. The next ridge reveals that the destination lies beyond another unexpected valley.
2. A narrow saddle concentrates all practical routes through the area.
3. Loose rock breaks free on a steep descent.
4. A sheltered hollow contains signs of repeated camps.
5. A hilltop marker or ruin provides a strong navigation point.
6. Distant travelers are visible on a parallel ridge.
7. A stream has cut a deeper gully than expected across the route.
8. A low cloud or ordinary mist obscures the highest ground without becoming a Fog overlay.
9. An animal trail contours around a slope more efficiently than the direct climb.
10. A rock shelf provides excellent shelter but limited escape routes.
11. A landslip scar reveals unstable ground ahead without assuming an active disaster overlay.
12. The party must choose between an exposed ridge route and a longer valley route.

## 6. Canyon / Badlands

**Preset:** `ENVPRESET-DL-CANYON`  
**Primary archetype:** `ARCH-HIGHLAND`  
**Secondary archetype:** `ARCH-OPEN-COUNTRY`

### Overview

Strongly eroded terrain of canyons, ravines, gullies, mesas, hoodoos and broken exposed ground. Route restriction and vertical separation matter as much as distance; nearby destinations can require long detours to find safe descents or crossings.

### Environmental features

- steep walls, narrow ravines, dry washes and branching gullies;
- mesas, fins, pillars and eroded ridges;
- loose sediment, exposed rock and sparse vegetation;
- natural arches, ledges, alcoves and chokepoints;
- intermittent watercourses where locally present.

### Movement and navigation

- line-of-sight distance can be misleading because vertical barriers force detours;
- canyon floors may provide corridors but can branch or dead-end;
- safe ascent/descent points are valuable route nodes;
- rim travel offers visibility but increases fall/exposure risk;
- flash flooding requires an active hydrology/weather state and is not assumed in baseline conditions.

### Hazards

- falls, rockfall and unstable edges;
- loose scree or crumbly sediment;
- dead-end routes and difficult reversals;
- limited water and shade in dry instances;
- narrow channels that become dangerous only when later flood/storm conditions apply.

### Encounters and challenges

Use blocked ravines, hidden alcoves, ancient routes, bridge points, high-ground observers, water seeps, exposed fossils/materials, wildlife signs near scarce resources, and route conflicts at the few practical crossings.

### Rest and shelter

Alcoves, overhangs, side canyons and shaded walls can provide strong shelter. Canyon floors should be assessed for drainage before use; a potential flash-flood hazard is conditional rather than permanently active.

### Random encounters (d12)

1. The chosen ravine narrows into an impassable slot.
2. A hidden side canyon contains shade, water signs or an old camp.
3. Loose stone falls from a rim after movement above.
4. A natural arch or ledge creates a difficult but useful shortcut.
5. Tracks converge on a rare seep beneath an overhang.
6. A mesa-top route offers a clear view but no easy descent.
7. Erosion has destroyed a remembered trail or crossing.
8. A rockfall partially blocks the canyon floor.
9. Distant movement appears briefly on the rim and vanishes.
10. Exposed layers reveal a buried object, fossil or resource seam.
11. A narrow passage forces groups traveling opposite directions to negotiate right of way.
12. The route splits between a long safe detour and a risky direct climb.

## 7. Rocky Desert

**Preset:** `ENVPRESET-DL-ROCKY-DESERT`  
**Primary archetype:** `ARCH-OPEN-COUNTRY`  
**Secondary archetype:** `ARCH-HIGHLAND`

### Overview

Arid exposed country dominated by gravel, stone pavement, rock fields, mesas, escarpments and broken dry relief rather than extensive loose sand. It complements the existing Sandy Desert preset instead of replacing it.

### Environmental features

- gravel plains, bare rock, boulder fields and stony washes;
- mesas, buttes, low escarpments and isolated rock towers;
- sparse vegetation concentrated around cracks, washes or water sources;
- long visibility interrupted by abrupt rocky relief;
- dry channels and erosion surfaces.

### Movement and navigation

- firm rock or gravel may support efficient travel until broken by boulders, ledges or gullies;
- landmarks are often strong but can appear deceptively close;
- vehicle/mount suitability is external and must be evaluated against actual substrate and relief;
- loose sand is not assumed; that belongs to Sandy Desert or local patches;
- extreme heat, drought and dust storms remain overlays/local facts.

### Hazards

- sharp or unstable rock, scree and hidden drop-offs;
- scarce dependable water;
- strong exposure with little shade outside rock formations;
- difficult footing through boulder fields;
- ordinary dust and glare without assuming severe environmental overlays.

### Encounters and challenges

Use scarce springs, abandoned mining or travel sites, rock shelters, dry washes, animal trails, routefinding between mesas, distant mirages or glare effects without supernatural assumptions, and resource competition around shade/water.

### Rest and shelter

Rock overhangs, canyon walls, boulder clusters and built structures can provide excellent shade and wind protection. Low dry washes may be sheltered but should be evaluated for later storm/flood risk.

### Random encounters (d12)

1. A boulder field makes the direct route much slower than it appeared.
2. A shaded overhang contains signs of repeated use.
3. A dry wash cuts a natural corridor through broken ground.
4. A distant mesa serves as a strong landmark but remains farther away than expected.
5. Fresh tracks lead toward a narrow rock cleft.
6. Loose scree shifts on a slope or escarpment.
7. An abandoned camp or work site still contains useful structural shelter.
8. A small spring or seep is hidden at the base of rock.
9. Glare makes a route or object difficult to judge at distance.
10. Erosion exposes a mineral seam, fossil layer or buried object.
11. Travelers converge on the same shaded pass between rock formations.
12. The party must choose between a level long route and a shorter broken climb.

## 8. Salt Flats

**Preset:** `ENVPRESET-DL-SALT-FLATS`  
**Primary archetype:** `ARCH-OPEN-COUNTRY`

### Overview

Extremely open basin floors or dry lakebeds covered by salt crust, mineral deposits or hard pale sediment. The terrain is visually simple but can conceal weak crust, brine pockets, mud beneath the surface and severe exposure.

### Environmental features

- broad pale crust or polygonal mineral surfaces;
- almost no natural vertical cover across the central flat;
- distant mountain, ridge or basin-edge landmarks;
- occasional brine pools, wet patches, salt ridges or mineral mounds;
- reflective surface glare where lighting conditions support it.

### Movement and navigation

- firm crust can allow very direct travel;
- distance and scale are difficult to judge across featureless horizons;
- weak crust, damp sections or hidden mud can abruptly change footing;
- reliable navigation may depend on distant landmarks or instruments owned by other systems;
- heat, cold, storms and extreme brightness remain overlays/local conditions rather than universal baseline effects.

### Hazards

- deceptive crust strength;
- caustic/mineral exposure only where locally/source established, not assumed universally;
- lack of shade and shelter;
- scarce potable water;
- glare and featureless navigation;
- sudden soft ground around brine or seasonal wet zones.

### Encounters and challenges

Use abandoned vehicles or camps, tracks that cross apparently empty ground, brine pools, mineral harvest sites, distant travelers, navigation errors, weak crust fields, unusual acoustic/visual distance effects and resource caches near basin edges.

### Rest and shelter

Natural shelter on the flat itself is minimal. Basin edges, isolated rock, vehicles, tents or built structures are valuable. A stable-looking crust should not be assumed suitable for heavy loads without local confirmation.

### Random encounters (d12)

1. The crust fractures under weight and reveals damp mud beneath.
2. A distant object appears close but takes far longer to reach.
3. Tracks cross the flat with no visible traveler in either direction.
4. A shallow brine pool interrupts the straight route.
5. Wind has built a low ridge of salt or mineral debris.
6. An abandoned camp or vehicle provides the only visible shelter.
7. Glare briefly hides a basin-edge landmark used for navigation.
8. A firm route narrows between several weak-crust patches.
9. Mineral harvesting or survey markers show recent organized activity.
10. A dry playa margin becomes unexpectedly soft and rutted.
11. Distant travelers alter course toward the same isolated landmark.
12. The direct route remains open, but all known water and shelter lie on a longer basin-edge path.
