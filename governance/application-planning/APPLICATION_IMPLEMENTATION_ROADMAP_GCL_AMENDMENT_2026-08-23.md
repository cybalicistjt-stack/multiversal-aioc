## Purpose

## Critical-path preservation

This amendment does **not** change the currently selected application implementation work item or interrupt the active MSS implementation sequence.

GCL is a parallel content/design track. It may be executed independently when the owner explicitly selects GCL, but planning or executing GCL does not mark application tranches complete, does not grant application-code implementation authority, and does not pause or supersede MSS/CCP/DPL/MAI/AAI/ISE/WCI or later critical-path work.

## Product decision

Multiversal should rarely require a GM to begin from a blank page. The internal construction library may become very large, but the normal user experience must remain intent-first rather than catalog-first.

The GM should be able to ask for a need such as:

- a complete encounter or scene;
- a hook or complication;
- an easier/harder version;
- an alternative route after unexpected player action;
- a mystery, heist, rescue, political crisis or other structure;
- a short/session/campaign architecture;
- a source-respecting creature/adversary role conversion;
- a genre/tone transformation;
- a compatible replacement for one component of an existing construction.

Search, tags and expert browsing remain available, but giant undifferentiated lists are not the primary UX.

## Difficulty/scaling rule

GCL must not introduce a universal Challenge Rating or reduce difficulty to HP/damage scaling. Difficulty shaping is advisory and multi-dimensional, including action economy, survivability, output, resources, range/mobility, environment, objectives, timers, information, hazards, waves, alternatives and consequences where source/rules context supports them.

Creature/adversary scaling should prefer meaningful role/capability transformations over raw stat multiplication and must not invent unsupported powers or boss-only rules merely to reach a desired strength.
